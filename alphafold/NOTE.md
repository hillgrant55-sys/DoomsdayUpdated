# The AlphaFold case: already answered, now tracked

Written 16 November 2026 after a literature check. No test run; no protocol attempt used.

**Question.** When a machine can do a research task people used to do, does it replace them or work alongside them? AlphaFold2 (July 2021) is the clearest case: predicted structures for hundreds of thousands of proteins appeared at once, at near-experimental accuracy.

**What is known.**
- An NBER working paper, "How Artificial Intelligence Shapes Science: Evidence from AlphaFold" (w35143), finds the rate of experimental structure determination almost unchanged after release, with predicted structures used to support experimental work. Basic research on proteins that had no structure before AlphaFold rose 15–40% relative to proteins that already had one.
- A second study, using 530,495 proteins linked to the PDB and differences in prediction quality, reports that AlphaFold2 increased experimental activity.
- A 2026 preprint tracking 245,396 PDB structures reports that a long decline in the study of novel proteins halted after 2021.
- Kovalevskiy, Mateos-Garcia and Tunyasuvunakool (PNAS 121: e2315002121, 2024; from Google DeepMind, so not independent) review uses by experimental structural biologists.

**Decision.** These studies use protein-level data we would have to rebuild, and they agree on the answer so far: complement, not substitute. Replicating them would add little. Instead the question is tracked as scoreboard entry S4 in `PREDICTIONS.md`, which will show whether the answer changes as prediction tools improve.
